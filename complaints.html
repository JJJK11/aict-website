Complaints & Appeals

AICT Global Services maintains transparent, accessible, and fair processes for handling complaints and appeals, in accordance with ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015 requirements.

Complaints About AICT

Any person or organization may file a complaint about AICT Global Services, including concerns about our certification activities, the conduct of our personnel or auditors, our policies or procedures, or any other matter related to our operations as a certification body.

Complaints may be submitted by certified clients, applicant organizations, users of certification (such as purchasers or regulators), members of the public, or any other interested party.

Complaints About a Certified Client

If you have a complaint about an organization that holds certification issued by AICT, you may report your concern to us. While AICT cannot intervene in the internal affairs of a certified organization, we are obligated to investigate complaints that relate to the conformity of the certified client's management system to the applicable standard.

Examples of complaints about certified clients include concerns about the validity of a certification claim, misuse of a certification mark, or evidence that a certified management system does not conform to the requirements of the standard.

How to File a Complaint

Complaints should be submitted in writing to ensure clarity and completeness. Please include the following information:

  • Your name and contact information
  • The organization or individual the complaint relates to
  • A clear description of the issue or concern
  • Any supporting evidence or documentation
  • The outcome you are seeking, if applicable

Submit your complaint to: complaints@aictglobalservices.com

Complaints Process

AICT handles complaints through a structured, documented process:

  1. Receipt and Acknowledgment
    AICT acknowledges receipt of your complaint within five (5) business days. We assign a unique reference number and identify the appropriate person to manage the complaint, ensuring that person was not involved in the subject of the complaint.
  2. Initial Assessment
    We assess whether the complaint falls within the scope of our complaints process and whether sufficient information has been provided. If additional information is needed, we will contact you. We determine whether the complaint relates to AICT's certification activities, a certified client's management system, or another matter.
  3. Investigation
    The complaint is investigated by gathering and evaluating relevant information and evidence. For complaints about certified clients, this may include reviewing audit records, contacting the certified organization, or scheduling additional audit activities. All parties are given an opportunity to provide their perspective.
  4. Resolution and Decision
    Based on the investigation findings, AICT determines the appropriate resolution. This may include corrective actions, changes to certification status, process improvements, or a determination that the complaint is not substantiated. The decision is communicated to the complainant and relevant parties.
  5. Communication of Outcome
    AICT formally communicates the outcome of the complaint to you, including the rationale for the decision and any actions taken. If you are dissatisfied with the outcome, you may escalate the matter as described below.

AICT endeavors to resolve complaints within thirty (30) business days from the date of receipt. If a complaint requires extended investigation, we will inform you of the expected timeline.

Appeals Process

An appeal is a formal request to reconsider a certification decision made by AICT. Appeals may be filed by certified clients or applicant organizations who disagree with a decision regarding their certification, including decisions to grant, refuse, maintain, renew, suspend, restore, or withdraw certification.

Grounds for Appeal

Appeals should be based on substantive grounds, such as:

  • Errors in the assessment of evidence during the audit
  • Failure to follow established certification procedures
  • Incorrect interpretation of standard requirements
  • New evidence that was not available at the time of the certification decision
  • Procedural irregularities that may have affected the outcome

Filing an Appeal

Appeals must be submitted in writing within thirty (30) calendar days of receiving the certification decision. The appeal should clearly state the decision being appealed, the grounds for appeal, any supporting evidence, and the outcome sought.

Submit your appeal to: complaints@aictglobalservices.com with the subject line "Certification Appeal".

Appeal Review

Appeals are reviewed by individuals who were not involved in the original certification decision. The review examines the original audit evidence, the certification decision rationale, the grounds cited in the appeal, and any new evidence provided. The appellant is given an opportunity to present their case. AICT endeavors to resolve appeals within forty-five (45) business days.

Confidentiality

All complaints and appeals are treated confidentially. Information related to complaints and appeals is disclosed only to those persons who need to know in order to process and resolve the matter. The identity of the complainant is protected to the extent possible, consistent with the need to conduct a fair and thorough investigation.

Escalation to the Standards Council of Canada

If you are not satisfied with the outcome of AICT's complaints or appeals process, you have the right to escalate the matter to the Standards Council of Canada (SCC). SCC, as Canada's national accreditation body, has the authority to investigate complaints against certification bodies operating under its accreditation framework.

Information about how to file a complaint with SCC is available on the SCC website (www.scc.ca).
